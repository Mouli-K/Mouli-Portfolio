PROJECT REF: 2023HT30668 • BITS PILANI DISSERTATION

Multi-Domain Optimization of Automotive Leaf Springs

Via Structural Geometry, Joint Architecture, and Hybrid Material Integration (In collaboration with LTTS)

Download 58-Page Thesis (PDF)

Executive Problem Statement & Methodology

Conventional multi-leaf automotive suspension springs in 55Si7/65Si7 spring steel are heavy and prone to inter-leaf friction and fatigue wear. This research explored the simultaneous optimization of cross-sectional geometry, eye-joint architecture, and advanced fiber-reinforced polymer composites to achieve dramatic mass reduction without compromising suspension load-carrying capacity.

A total of 75 distinct geometric and material configurations were modeled and evaluated using 3D finite element simulation and a systematic Weighted Pugh Decision Matrix. The material candidates investigated included structural spring steel (65Si7), E-glass/epoxy, S-glass/epoxy, Carbon/epoxy, and novel hybrid sandwich topologies.

PROJECT REF: MEC 300 • SASTRA DEEMED UNIVERSITY

Effect of Laser Shock Peening on ZE41 Mg Alloy

Microstructural Characterization and Mechanical Tensile Property Enhancement

Download 39-Page Report (PDF)

Surface Mechanics & Dislocation Density Engineering

Magnesium alloys like ZE41 (Mg-Zn-RE-Zr) are among the lightest structural metals for aerospace gearbox casings and automotive powertrain brackets, but their low yield strength and fatigue susceptibility restrict widespread adoption.

This experimental research investigated Laser Shock Peening (LSP) without coating using a high-intensity pulsed Nd:YAG laser. High-amplitude shock waves (in the GPa range) propagated through the material, driving severe plastic deformation, high dislocation density, and deep compressive residual stress fields.

PROJECT REF: MEC 400 • SASTRA MAJOR PROJECT

PLA Reinforced Coir Fibre Polymer Composite via Stir Casting

Experimental Analysis of Mechanical Properties for Eco-Friendly Lightweight Components

Download 45-Page Report (PDF)

Fabricated biodegradable green composites by reinforcing a Polylactic Acid (PLA) bio-thermoplastic matrix with chemically treated natural coir fibers. Investigated the influence of fiber surface alkali treatment (NaOH solution) to eliminate hemicellulose, lignin, and surface waxes, drastically improving mechanical interfacial bonding between fiber and matrix.

Utilized an adapted stir casting apparatus with controlled heating mantle and mechanical impeller stirring to ensure homogeneous fiber dispersion. Specimen coupons were subjected to standard ASTM testing for tensile strength (ASTM D638), flexural modulus (ASTM D790), and Izod impact resistance (ASTM D256).
